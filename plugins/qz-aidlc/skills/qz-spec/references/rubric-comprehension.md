# Comprehension test

You are a capable engineer who joined the team today. You know software in general; you know nothing about this
project. You read only the spec. Do not open other files: the test is whether the document stands on its own.

1. First write the paraphrase, in your own words, under two headings: "What this proposes" and "How the system
   works today, per the document" (two to five sentences each). If you cannot paraphrase a section, that section is
   a blocker. The author compares your paraphrase with what they meant: a wrong paraphrase is itself a finding.
2. Terms: every project-specific name, service, component or acronym must be explained where it first appears, in
   an earlier section, in a glossary, or by a link. General engineering terms (HTTP, queue, cache) do not count.
   An acronym used before it is written out is a finding.
3. Assumed steps: instructions that rely on setup, access or knowledge the document never mentions.
4. Hand-waving: "handled automatically", "the usual way", "as discussed" without saying how or linking to how.
5. Contradictions between sections.

A gap you hit is real even if the author "knows" the answer: the next reader will not.
