# Meeting preparation · the deck is a third of it

Write this as one page beside the deck (`meeting-prep.md` in the project). It is internal: it never goes to the
client and may name what the deck must not.

## 1. People
| Who | Role in the decision | Cares about | Likely objection | What we want from them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| name or role | decides / influences / can block / uses it | in their words | the sentence they will say | approve, sponsor, give access, stay neutral |

Write for the decider. Prepare for the blocker. Give the user something concrete to react to.

## 2. Decisions needed
List them as questions with a yes or no answer, in the order you will ask. One meeting, one main decision. If you
need three, say which one is today.

## 3. Assumptions to confirm
Each thing the proposal depends on that the client has not said: access, environments, licences, who approves
gates, data that may leave their network. For each: who can confirm it, and what changes if it is false.

## 4. The hard questions
Run `qz-deck-rehearsal`. It returns the questions a sceptical executive would ask, most damaging first. For each,
decide one of:
- **Answer on a slide**: the deck was missing something.
- **Backup slide**: a good answer that would slow the main story.
- **Say it in the notes**: a one-line answer the presenter has ready.
- **"We do not know yet"**: with what we will do to find out, and by when. This is a strong answer when it is true.

The ones that come up in most rooms:
1. What happens when it is wrong? Who is accountable?
2. What does our team have to stop doing to make room for this?
3. What have you actually run, and what are you assuming?
4. What does it cost after the pilot, and what drives that cost?
5. What leaves our network, and who can see it?
6. Why you, and why not the tool vendor or our own team?
7. What do we own at the end, and can we run it without you?
8. What is the earliest point we can stop, and what will we have by then?

## 5. Do not commit to
- A date before discovery has confirmed scope and access.
- A fixed price for work that is not yet specified.
- A productivity number on their work. Offer a target and the way it will be measured.
- Anything about their security, compliance or data that their own team has not confirmed.
- Staff by name, or onshore presence that has not been priced.
- Results "as at another client". Say what we will build and how it will be checked here.

If asked for one of these in the room: "I would rather give you a number I can stand behind. We will have it by
<date>, after <what>."

## 6. Risks to the meeting itself
The decider leaves early (so the ask is on the first three slides too). The demo fails (have the recording). Someone
takes the meeting into detail (offer the backup slide and a follow-up). A new stakeholder appears (ask what they
need to see).

## 7. Rehearse
Out loud, with a clock, once. Cut until it fits in two thirds of the slot. Decide who presents, who answers
technical questions, and who takes notes. Open the deck and the demo on the machine and the screen that will be
used.

## 8. Afterwards, within a day
Send: the decision as you understood it, what each side does next with a date and a name, the answers you promised,
and the deck. Record open questions and commitments in the project so the next person does not make them again.
Sending anything to the client waits for the person's yes on the exact text and recipients.
